# Introduction
The morning bells of Vilgeismar begin before the sun has fully cleared the eastern hills, their iron throats carrying across the stone roofs and narrow streets with a resonance that feels heavier than it once did. The sound travels more slowly now, as though the air itself has thickened during the long weeks of siege. It moves over shuttered windows, along battlements blackened by fire, and through courtyards where frost gathers in thin silver crusts upon empty barrels and unused carts.

Above the city, the veil remains. In the early light it is almost invisible, a distortion more than a barrier, like the faint shimmer of heat above summer stone. As the sun rises further, its surface catches the light and reveals a pale iridescence stretched in a wide dome over Vilgeismar’s walls.

The color is not constant. It shifts subtly when observed too long — pearl becoming dull silver, silver deepening toward something faintly opaline. It does not flicker. It does not waver. It simply exists, curving across the sky where clouds should move freely.

Within the city, life resumes with the slow reluctance of the underfed. The air carries the mingled scents of damp stone, ash, old smoke, and the sour tang of boiled leather. Somewhere near Beggar’s Court, water sloshes into wooden buckets drawn from the central well. The sound is steady and practical, followed by the scrape of ladles against the sides of ration cauldrons. There is no shouting in the ration lines this morning, only the low murmur of voices that conserve their strength. 

Hunger has a smell of its own, rot, metallic and faintly sweet and it clings to clothing and breath alike. From the Cathedral of Twelve Faces comes the muted echo of choral rehearsal. The
doors stand open, and candlelight glows against the stone interior despite the growing daylight. The hymn is familiar, disciplined. It threads through the streets nearby in careful harmony, mournful, and deliberate. The final verse lingers a fraction longer than the others before resolving into the next stanza, they can never stop.
Seven days have passed since the night the outer gate splintered and the defenders faltered. 

Seven days since the High Lady and the Sisters gathered beneath the vaulted ceiling and sang as the enemy pressed through smoke and flame.


Seven days since the sky above Vilgeismar took on its present form and the besieging army withdrew beyond sight of the walls.
The famine did not leave with them. The granaries remain hollow. The butcher stalls stand empty. Livestock bones lie
bleached in refuse pits. Those who move through the streets do so with careful economy, their steps measured, their gestures spare. Children no longer run; they walk beside their elders with solemn attention, as though conserving breath is an instinct rather than instruction. 

A messenger of the Cloister moves through the city at midmorning, her cloak marked with the sigil of the Many Faced Lady. She walks with upright posture despite the sharpness of her collarbones beneath the fabric. In her hands she carries sealed writs stamped in wax. She does not speak as she delivers them. She waits only long enough to see each seal accepted before moving on. 

The parchment is thick. The wax bears the unmistakable mark of the High Lady. The summons within is concise and formal, requesting immediate attendance at a private council within the Cathedral grounds and instructing discretion in all matters related to it.


## Starving Vilgeismar

For four months, the castle-city of Vilgeismar has endured a full military siege. The surrounding farms were burned early in the campaign, trade routes were severed, and supply caravans never arrived. The granaries have been emptied, livestock slaughtered, and every viable reserve rationed to exhaustion. Hunger has become the defining condition of daily life.

On the night the outer gate was breached and the defending forces began to fail, the High Lady and the Sisters of the Cloister gathered in the Cathedral of Twelve Faces and performed the
Hymn of Twelve Faces in full choral prayer. During the final verse, a pale, luminous veil formed over the city and extended beyond the walls. Enemy forces attempting to advance reported terror, disorientation, and physical resistance. By dawn, the besieging army had withdrawn beyond visible range. 

The barrier remains in place. It prevents hostile approach and deflects projectiles and fire. It does not provide food, restore the dead, or repair the damage already done.

The people of Vilgeismar refer to this event simply as the Miracle.


## The Many Faced Lady

The patron deity of Vilgeismar is the Many Faced Lady, a monotheistic god understood
through multiple aspects. She is venerated as protector, judge, comforter, witness, and guide.
The theology of the city holds that the Many Faced Lady does not intervene directly in mortal
affairs except under extraordinary circumstances. Devotion is expressed through discipline,
confession, endurance, and communal ritual rather than expectation of divine rescue.
The Miracle has prompted renewed fervor. Many interpret the barrier as proof that the Lady
intervenes when faith is unified and suffering is borne with humility. Others consider it a sign
of impending judgment, purification, or transformation.
Official doctrine maintains that the Miracle is an act of divine protection granted in response
to collective prayer. There are twelve widely accepted incarnations of her:

## Conditions Within

## the Veil

The barrier has secured Vilgeismar from
external assault but has not resolved the
famine. Rations remain scarce. Leather has
been boiled for sustenance. Draft animals
and pets have long since been consumed.
Starvation-related illness is widespread.
Water remains available from the central
well. It is clear and plentiful, though some
residents report that it tastes unusually
sweet or metallic. No formal investigation
has confirmed a cause.
Daily life is structured around:
● Strict ration distribution
● Increased attendance at prayer
services
● Public confessions and communal
hymns
● Enforcement against hoarding
● Military patrols maintaining order
Despite hunger, widespread rioting has not
occurred. The city remains disciplined,
though visibly strained.

Current Concern:

**Garran Thrice-Garrotted** operates from
the shadows of Butcher’s Row and the poorer
quarters. Once a smuggler, he has adapted
swiftly to famine. He controls illicit trade in
hidden food supplies, including meat of
uncertain origin, and his enforcers ensure that
questions are not asked publicly. Rumors
whisper that some of his product comes from
the unclaimed dead or from those who “vanish”
from Beggar’s Court. Garran frames his
enterprise as survival, claiming that he merely
provides what the city refuses to acknowledge
it needs. He profits as hunger deepens, and the
longer the siege endures, the stronger his
position becomes.
**Old Marta Kell** , a widow of Beggar’s Court,
has no official title but commands quiet respect
among commoners. Having lost two sons
during the siege, she speaks plainly and
without reverence for hierarchy. She insists the
air tastes different since the Miracle and keeps
careful track of who disappears from her
district. Though she does not openly defy the
Cathedral, she trusts lived experience more
than proclamation and may push others to
confront truths they would rather ignore.

city?
